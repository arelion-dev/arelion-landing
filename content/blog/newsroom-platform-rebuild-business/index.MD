A legacy CMS nobody fully understands vetoes every idea. Analytics, personalisation, subscriptions, a related-articles rail that works: each one dies the moment it touches the platform. I move publishers onto a platform they own, without downtime a reader can see.

**For a national news outlet: 500,000+ articles moved with zero reader-facing downtime. Editors paused publishing for about thirty minutes during cutover, and that was the whole visible cost.**

## Where the time goes today

Engineering avoids the archive because touching it is risky, so every product idea waits. The migration keeps getting postponed too, because a bad one fails in public: a big copy runs for hours while the source keeps changing, something does not match, and the site breaks during the exact window everyone is watching.

## What changes

- **Readers keep reading.** The site serves traffic every second of the move: tens of millions of pageviews a month, and every registered reader carried over with none locked out.
- **Proven before it flips.** An automated comparison replays real traffic against the old and new systems until they answer identically, down to a single character.
- **Every step can be undone.** I move in small pieces, each one reversible in about a minute, because the old system stays frozen and available as the way back.
- **A platform you own.** Your team controls the archive and the stack, instead of waiting on a vendor.

One limit: the move builds none of the new products. It makes analytics and subscriptions possible, and building them is separate work.
