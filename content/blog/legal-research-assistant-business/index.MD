I designed and built a legal-research assistant over a jurisdiction's legislation. It answers questions about the law and puts a clickable footnote on each point.

The risk it removes is simple: a legal chatbot that invents case law is worse than no chatbot at all. A lawyer who reads "Article 12 of Decree No. 4 of 2022" in a confident paragraph acts on it. If that article does not exist, or the number is off by one, the tool has planted a landmine in someone's advice.

The catch is that a wrong answer looks exactly like a right one. In a regulated field, one fabricated citation, seen once, ends trust in the whole product.

So this assistant is built so it cannot cite a law it does not have in front of it. Every citation is checked against the laws the system just retrieved and against the article text on file. If a reference does not verify, it is stripped before you ever see it. Each footnote resolves to a real law with a real source url from an official portal.

And when it cannot confirm something, it says so: no matching text, an article number it will not stand behind, a jurisdiction outside its coverage. In this field a clear "I cannot confirm this" is worth far more than a confident guess.
