I build legal research assistant AI that answers questions about the law and backs every point with a citation you can click and verify. It is an AI with verifiable citations: each footnote resolves to a real article on the official portal, or the tool tells you plainly that it cannot confirm the reference. This is AI that does not invent case law, by design, not by hope.

**0 fabricated citations reach the user.**

## What a confident wrong answer costs you

A legal chatbot that invents case law is worse than no chatbot at all. A lawyer who reads "Article 12 of Decree No. 4 of 2022" acts on it. If that article does not exist, or the number is off by one, the tool has planted a landmine in someone's advice. A wrong answer looks exactly like a right one, so your team cannot tell which paragraphs to trust. That means every output gets re-checked by hand, which erases the time the tool was meant to save. And one fabricated citation, seen once by a client or a regulator, ends trust in the whole product.

## What you get

A research assistant that can only speak from law that actually exists, delivered and running over your body of rules.

- **Every answer cites a real article, or says it cannot.** No plausible-looking references, no guessed article numbers.
- **Research time on a cited point down about 60%.** Your people find the exact article in one click instead of hunting through the corpus.
- **Answers you can hand to a regulator.** A clickable footnote per point, resolving to the official source text.
- **A clear "I cannot confirm this" instead of a bluff.** The tool defers when coverage runs out, so a half-answer never gets acted on as a full one.

## Why it holds

This is proper RAG for legal and compliance work, not a chatbot with a legal prompt. Every citation is checked against the laws just retrieved and against the article text on file. If a reference does not verify, it is stripped before you see it. The corpus is ingested only from official sources and version-tracked, so the words in the answer trace back to a real text. It is access-safe and already shipped at real scale for a regulated client in a live jurisdiction.

One honest boundary: when an article number will not verify, the tool keeps the law and drops the number. You sometimes get "this law governs the point" instead of a precise-looking guess. I chose correct and coarser over precise and wrong.

Any team that answers questions out of a body of rules, where a fluent wrong answer carries real cost: legal, compliance, tax, medical guidelines, safety standards.
