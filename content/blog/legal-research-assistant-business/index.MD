A lawyer needs the exact article they can put in front of a regulator. "The tool said so" is not an answer a regulator accepts. So I designed and built a legal-research assistant over a jurisdiction's legislation. It answers with a clickable footnote on each point, and every footnote resolves to a real law.

**0 fabricated citations reach the user.**

## The expensive failure

A legal chatbot that invents case law is worse than no chatbot at all. A lawyer who reads "Article 12 of Decree No. 4 of 2022" acts on it. If that article does not exist, or the number is off by one, the tool has planted a landmine in someone's advice. A wrong answer looks exactly like a right one, so one fabricated citation, seen once, ends trust in the whole product.

## What this does differently

The assistant cannot cite a law it does not have in front of it. Every citation is checked against the laws it just retrieved and against the article text on file. If a reference does not verify, it is stripped before you see it.

**Every answer cites a real article, or says it cannot.**

When it cannot confirm something, it says so, rather than guess. A clear "I cannot confirm this" beats a confident wrong answer. **Research time on a cited point is down about 60%.**
