A national news outlet had twelve years of coverage, more than 600,000 articles, sitting in a database doing nothing. I built the machinery that turns that archive into something a company pays for.

Here is the shift. An archive that size is not content, it is a dataset. And a dataset about a country's politics has buyers who are not readers: lobbyists, compliance teams, embassies, investors. They pay many times what a reader pays, from a company budget.

The model is already proven. Politico Pro took a beat the newsroom already covered and sold it to people whose job depends on it. It is now roughly half of Politico's revenue, around $15,000 per contract, with 93% renewal.

I built three layers. Structure, so you can finally ask the archive a question. Monitoring, so it stays current on its own. And an analyst layer that drafts the trackers and briefings, with every line cited back to the source. Your journalists validate and sign off, so it carries their name, not a chatbot's.

The same shape fits anyone sitting on years of proprietary work: a trade publisher, a professional body, a market research firm, a legal publisher. The test is one question. Is there someone whose job depends on what you know, with a budget behind them?

If yes, your archive is not a storage cost, it is an unlaunched product. Want me to look at yours, in writing?
