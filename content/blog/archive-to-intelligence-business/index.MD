A national news outlet held over a decade of the national political record in one place and could not ask it a single question. Now "everything this person said about this policy since launch" comes back in under a second, with a citation on every row.

That is what it takes to monetize a content archive. Years of published work are not content. They are a dataset, and a dataset has buyers who never read your articles. I build the machinery that turns a dead archive into structured, queryable records, then into intelligence products your commercial team can sell.

## Your archive is a cost line pretending to be a library

500,000+ articles. Most tags used on a single article, tens of thousands untagged, "related articles" returning noise. Tens of millions of monthly pageviews out front, and the archive behind them earned once, at publication, then never again.

You pay to store a record nobody can question. Every year it gets bigger and it earns exactly the same: nothing.

## What you get

- **A dead archive becomes a queryable record.** Every article turned into structured rows: who said what, which vote, which seat, which statement, resolved across the whole corpus. **Half a day of manual digging becomes under a second, with a citation per row.**
- **It stays current with no data-entry headcount.** New articles land already structured. The tags problem dies as a side effect, which also repairs "related articles" for ordinary readers.
- **A new product line your team can sell.** A weekly political tracker, regulatory monitoring, sector briefings, election intelligence. Junior-analyst work drafted by the machine, every draft cited to its source, signed off by your own people so it carries their name.

## Why this holds

- **Every draft cites its source.** No chatbot claims, no invented facts. Your people validate and add the judgement clients pay for.
- **Access-safe, and already shipped at real scale** for a national news outlet, on the full archive, not a demo.
- **The model is documented, not a bet.** Politico Pro took a beat the newsroom already covered and sold it to people whose job depends on it: roughly half of Politico's revenue, around $15,000 per contract, about 90% renewal.
