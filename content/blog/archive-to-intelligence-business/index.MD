A national news outlet kept over a decade of the country's political record in one place, and couldn't ask it a single question. Now "everything this person said about this policy since launch" comes back in under a second, with a citation on every row.

That's what it takes to monetize a content archive. Years of published work add up to a dataset, and a dataset has buyers who'll never read your articles. I build the machinery that turns a dead archive into structured, queryable records, and then into intelligence products your commercial team can sell.

## Your archive is a cost line pretending to be a library

500,000+ articles. Most tags sat on a single article, tens of thousands had none, and "related articles" returned noise. Out front you've got tens of millions of pageviews a month; behind them sits an archive that earned once, at publication, and never again.

You're paying to store a record nobody can question. Every year it gets bigger, and every year it earns exactly the same: nothing.

## What you get

- **A dead archive becomes a queryable record.** Every article turns into structured rows (who said what, which vote, which seat, which statement) resolved across the whole corpus. **Half a day of manual digging becomes under a second, with a citation per row.**
- **It stays current with no data-entry headcount.** New articles land already structured, and the tags problem dies as a side effect, which also repairs "related articles" for your ordinary readers.
- **A new product line your team can sell.** A weekly political tracker, regulatory monitoring, sector briefings, election intelligence. The junior-analyst work gets drafted by the machine, every draft cited back to its source, then signed off by your own people so it carries their name.

## Why this holds

- **Every draft cites its source.** No chatbot claims, no invented facts. Your people validate and add the judgement clients pay for.
- **Access-safe, and already shipped at real scale** for a national news outlet, on the full archive.
- **The model is documented and proven.** Politico Pro took a beat the newsroom already covered and sold it to people whose job depends on it: roughly half of Politico's revenue, around $15,000 per contract, about 90% renewal.
