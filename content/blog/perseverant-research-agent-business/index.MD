Ask a chatbot to compare four product lines and it describes two, fluently, then stops. Someone then digs out the other two by hand, or nobody notices and a decision rides on half the data. I build research agents that answer every part of the question from your own documents, with each claim cited to the exact page. I shipped one for a global enterprise, over decades of its reports, patents and contracts, most of them scans nobody could search.

**Multi-part answers went from roughly 40% to 98% complete, with 0 silent half-answers in production (illustrative figures). Now used across 4 teams.**

## Where the time goes today

Hunting through documents takes hours, and a fluent half-answer makes it worse. A blank answer gets double-checked; a confident partial one gets trusted. The missing part surfaces weeks later, when someone reopens the file and redoes the work.

## What changes

- **The whole question, answered.** Ask about four things and get four sourced answers, including the ones it had to dig for.
- **A source on every line.** Every claim points to its page, or the agent says it found nothing. It never answers from memory.
- **Old scans made searchable.** Scans, born-digital files and a dozen languages become one corpus you can query.
- **Each person sees only what they are cleared for.** Access is enforced on the data itself.

The gain is measured: I can switch the diligence off, run the same graded questions both ways and compare. One limit: it is built for questions your documents can answer, and it tells you when they cannot.
