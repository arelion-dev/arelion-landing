I build second brains: one place where anyone in your organisation asks a question in plain language and gets an answer grounded in your own knowledge, with a citation. Your institution stops being hostage to what lives in one person's head. I built one for a national news outlet with a decade-old archive of 500,000+ articles, plus the operating knowledge spread across every desk.

## Why this matters to you

Your best knowledge leaves on a Friday. When a senior person resigns, a decade of context goes with them: who to call, what was already tried, why a process is the way it is. Nobody wrote it down, because it lived in their head. Every new hire then burns weeks chasing answers that already exist somewhere in the building, and every departure is a permanent loss. You're paying to rediscover the same knowledge over and over.

## What it costs when it walks out

The bill shows up twice. Onboarding runs for weeks because the answers live in people rather than systems, so every new hire interrupts colleagues to do the job. And when your most experienced person leaves, the judgement they built over years is gone, so you pay to relearn it, often by repeating a mistake someone already knew to avoid.

The tempting shortcut makes it worse. Point a generic chatbot at your documents and it will answer confidently even when it's wrong, and it will happily surface a restricted note to whoever asks. In a newsroom or a law firm, a confident wrong answer that looks sourced does more damage than no answer at all.

## What actually makes it trustworthy

What makes it trustworthy is boring and strict.

- **Grounded, or it says nothing.** Every answer comes from your own documents and carries a citation, so a claim is checkable. Where your records are silent, it says it found nothing instead of inventing.
- **Access-safe by design.** Clearance is enforced at retrieval time, per team, so a person only ever gets answers built from what they're allowed to read. A restricted note is never even a candidate.
- **One question box over everything.** Staff ask in plain language across published work and every team's internal knowledge, and get an answer in seconds instead of a half-day of asking around.
- **Onboarding in days.** A new hire asks the second brain instead of interrupting five colleagues.

One honest boundary: it captures what your people actually write down. The instinct nobody put into words still leaves with them. What changes is that far more becomes capturable, and there's finally a place worth writing it into.
