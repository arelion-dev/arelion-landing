A scientist needs one number from a 2009 study. It is buried in a scanned PDF nobody can find. She spends half a day hunting, gives up, and reruns the study. The company pays for that work twice.

I designed and built the engine that ends this, for a global beauty group: thirty brands, decades of R&D, more than 100 million pages, a dozen languages. The knowledge existed. Nobody could find it.

**Now the same question is answered in under a second, across all 100 million pages.**

That is the quiet, expensive failure I fixed. A constraint missed on page 40 of a dossier nobody reopened. A study redone because a scan stayed invisible. Confident half-answers that look finished and get acted on.

**Every sentence carries a clickable source, so checking costs one click instead of an afternoon.**

Two things make it safe to trust. It answers only from documents it can show you, never from memory, so every claim is checkable. And it searches only the slice you are cleared for: two people from two brands ask the same question and get two different answers, because access is enforced on the data, not promised in a policy.

The same problem shows up wherever an organization's knowledge outgrew its ability to search it: pharma dossiers, banking and KYC files, legal clause libraries, insurance claims in many formats. If your teams redo work that already exists, you have it.
