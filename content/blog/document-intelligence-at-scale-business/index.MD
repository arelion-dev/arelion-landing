I designed and built a document search engine for a global beauty group: thirty brands, decades of R&D, more than 100 million pages, in a dozen languages. The knowledge existed. Nobody could find it.

That is a quiet, expensive problem. A scientist burns half a day hunting a number asleep in a scanned PDF from 2009, gives up, and redoes the study. Work paid for twice. A regulatory constraint missed because it sat on page 40 of a dossier nobody reopened.

Now a person types a question in plain language and gets an answer in under a second, across all 100 million pages and thirty brands, with a clickable source on every sentence pointing at the exact page. Checking costs one click instead of an afternoon.

Two things make it safe to trust. It answers only from documents it can show you, never from memory, so every claim is checkable. And it only ever searches the slice of the corpus you are cleared for: two people from two brands asking the same question get two different answers, because access is enforced on the data, not promised in a policy.

The same problem shows up wherever an organization's knowledge outgrew its ability to search it: pharma and cosmetics dossiers, banking contracts and KYC files, legal clause libraries, insurance claims in several formats and languages. If your teams redo work that already exists, you have it.
