A scientist needs one number from a 2009 study. It sits in a scanned PDF nobody can find, so she spends half a day hunting, gives up and runs the study again. I build document AI that ends that search: a plain-language question, an answer in under a second across 100 million pages, and a source on every line that opens the page it came from.

**Live for a global enterprise: 100M+ pages, thirty brands, a dozen languages. Up to 40% fewer duplicated studies and re-reviews.**

## Where the time goes today

Every archive nobody can search is work you paid for once and pay for again. Studies get rerun, reviews get redone, and contract clauses get quoted from memory because the real one takes too long to find. In a regulated business, the constraint on page 40 of a dossier nobody reopens is also a risk you carry without knowing it.

## What changes

- **Seconds instead of half a day.** A one-line lookup comes back in under a second, with the source to check.
- **People check before they redo.** When the original is findable in seconds, the rerun stops.
- **Each team sees only its own documents.** Access is enforced inside the search itself, so two brands asking the same question get answers from their own files.
- **No answer without a source.** The system answers only from documents it can show you.

One limit: a deep question that cross-reads many documents takes minutes by design, and the interface says so instead of rushing a shallow answer.
