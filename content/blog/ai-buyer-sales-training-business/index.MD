New reps learn the hardest part of a sales call on real prospects, because there is nowhere else to practise. A junior meets a real objection for the first time on a lead your marketing paid for, freezes, and talks the buyer out of the deal. I build AI buyers for sales training: a realtime voice AI the rep practises against out loud, which resists the way a real prospect does and only opens up when approached well.

**Reps run around 40 practice calls before their first live prospect. Ramp time to a first closed deal drops about a third.**

## Where the time goes today

Practice costs either leads or manager hours. Role-play with a colleague falls apart once the pressure feels real, and a manager cannot sit in on every rep's practice. Call recordings only show the damage after it is done.

## What changes

- **Practice without burning leads.** The hard call happens on the AI buyer first.
- **Coaching without a meeting per rep.** Every call ends with a scored debrief that quotes the rep's own words, so a manager coaches the whole floor from the reports.
- **Scores that have to be earned.** Objection handling climbs from roughly 2.4 to 4.1 out of 5 in a rep's first month, against a buyer that hears hesitation and waits through a silence.
- **Your market, your personas.** You control how the buyer behaves, and no credential ever reaches a rep's browser, so it can go to a whole floor.

One limit: the score measures conversation craft against a rubric. It does not measure revenue, and it does not replace the senior who works a real room.
