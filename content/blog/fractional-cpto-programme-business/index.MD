A technical audit at a national news outlet flagged serious problems, then sat unactioned for weeks. Not because it was wrong. Because no single person owned the follow-through. I run the tech and product function of that outlet now as its fractional CTPO, and every one of those findings is actioned or scheduled.

**Audit findings actioned: from 0 to shipped.**

The company keeps its strategy. I own the roadmap, the technical decisions, and the team that ships them, on a programme contract rather than a salary. Most companies that stall on digital do not have a technology problem. They have an ownership problem: nobody owns product and technology as one accountable function. So audits sit unread and the product looks the same as it did ten years ago.

A vendor waits for instructions. An owner moves.

The work runs as five areas, delivered in phases: turn the data on, personalise, monetise, then compete. **Phase one is live before phase four starts.** Each phase pays on its own, so you never bet the whole budget on one promise a year out.

**Roadmap items with one owner: all of them.**

You get someone senior enough to sit across from the board and close enough to the work to make the calls directly. One name answers for the roadmap, the incidents, and the results, at a fraction of a C-level hire.
