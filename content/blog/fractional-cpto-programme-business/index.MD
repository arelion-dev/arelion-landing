I run the tech and product function of a national news outlet as its fractional CTPO. The company keeps its strategy. I own the roadmap, the technical decisions, and the team that ships them, on a programme contract rather than a salary.

Most companies that stall on digital do not have a technology problem. They have an ownership problem. The founders hold the vision, the teams do their jobs, and nobody owns product and technology as one accountable function. So audits sit unread, vendors deliver exactly what was asked and nothing more, and the product looks the same as it did ten years ago.

A vendor waits for instructions. An owner moves. That is the whole difference, and it is what this model buys you.

The work runs as five areas, delivered in phases: turn the data on, personalise, monetise, then compete. Each phase stands on its own and pays on its own, so you are never betting the whole budget on one big promise that lands in a year.

You get someone senior enough to sit across the table from the board, close enough to the work to make the calls directly, and contractually accountable for what ships. One name answers for the roadmap, the incidents, and the results. One throat to choke, at a fraction of a C-level hire and without the months of searching.

If your company runs on expertise your leaders carry in their heads, and you need it turned into products that ship, want me to look at your setup, in writing?
