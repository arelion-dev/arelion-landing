## An unowned audit is a bill you pay twice

You pay for the findings, then you pay again while nobody acts on them. That is what a missing tech and product function costs. The product looks the way it did a decade ago, and the archive you spent years building earns nothing.

Most companies that stall on digital do not have a technology problem. They have an ownership problem.

## What you get

**Audit findings actioned, from zero to shipped.** A report stops being a document and becomes work with a name on it.

**One name on every roadmap item, incident, and result.** When something breaks, the question is never whose job it is. It is already mine.

**A programme in five areas, phased so each pays alone.** Turn the data on, personalise, monetise, then compete. Phase one is live before phase four starts, so you never stake the whole budget on one promise a year out.

**Senior enough for the board, close enough to make the calls.** No three-week wait for a decision, at a fraction of a full C-level hire.

**A team shaped like the work, not an org chart.** Me, a dedicated engineer, and specialist capacity pulled in per phase, covering backend, frontend, mobile, data and AI, for less than the loaded cost of hiring the equivalent in-house.

## Why it holds

This is already shipped at real scale for a real client. Over a decade of articles, 500,000+ of them, consolidated into one queryable database. A rebuilt platform carrying tens of millions of monthly pageviews instead of fighting the product on top of it. Decisions land in days, not weeks, because one person answers for them.

One honest boundary: a fractional owner is a bet on one person's bandwidth and judgment, and the model is async, so I am not in the building for a fast corridor decision. I trade that reach for speed and single-point accountability on purpose. It is a real tradeoff, not a free lunch.
