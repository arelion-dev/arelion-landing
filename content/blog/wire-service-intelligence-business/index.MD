I build the AI layer that turns a news outlet's back archive into live, earning inventory, and takes the duplicated wire work off the desk. I did it for a national news outlet sitting on **over a decade and 500,000+ articles** nobody could reach, while readers left after **barely more than one page per visit**.

## Why this matters to you

Every published article is inventory you already own and already paid to produce. When nothing links into it, no reader loads it, so it earns nothing back. Meanwhile five editors keep rewriting the same wire dispatch that ten rival sites are running off the same feed, so you spend real money to produce a page identical to your competitors' and dead the day after it ships.

## What the buried archive costs you

A reader who sees one page and leaves is a reader you never showed a second ad to, on pages that cost nothing more to serve. That's the bill: ad inventory sitting idle because the archive is buried, and editorial hours burned on stories a reader can pull up anywhere. Reselling a commodity feed untouched is a race you lose on price.

## What actually moves the numbers

- **More impressions on inventory you already own.** Meaning-based internal linking resurfaces the right old article the moment it turns relevant again. In this deployment that meant a **double-digit lift in pages per visit**, so more page loads, more slots for your ad partner to sell, and no change to your ad stack.
- **The duplicated editorial work, gone.** When several agencies move on one event, the system collapses the near-identical wires into one, picks the strongest, and drafts a rewrite in your voice. **Five reads of one dispatch become one.** Editors put the saved hours into original work.
- **Your whole archive, queryable in plain language.** "What did we report on this MP the last time this came up?" stops being an afternoon of grepping and returns an answer in seconds, for the junior as readily as the veteran.

## What I can do

I build the technical layer that creates the owned inventory and saves the editorial time: meaning-based linking, wire clustering, and an in-CMS assistant that proposes but never publishes. I don't run header bidding, yield or any ad operations, and I don't touch your commercial setup. I hand your ad partner more inventory to sell, and they monetize it. Two boundaries stay firm: nothing publishes itself, and the journalist keeps the final click, because the byline is the whole asset.
