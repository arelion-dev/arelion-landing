Five editors open five wire dispatches on the same event, and five near-identical stories go live, the same ones ten rival sites are running off the same feed. You already paid to produce every article behind them, and most of that work is dead the day after it ships.

I build the AI layer that turns that back archive into live, earning inventory. I did it for a national news outlet sitting on **over a decade and 500,000+ articles** nobody could reach, while readers left after **barely more than one page per visit**.

## Your best inventory is the archive you stopped selling

Every published article is inventory you already own and already paid for. When nothing links into it, no reader ever loads it, so it earns you nothing.

A reader who sees one page and leaves is a reader you never showed a second ad to, on pages that cost you nothing more to serve. That's the bill: ad inventory sitting idle because the archive is buried.

## What you get

- **More impressions on inventory you already own.** Meaning-based internal linking resurfaces the right old article the moment it turns relevant again. In this deployment that meant **a double-digit lift in pages per visit**, so more page loads, more slots for your ad partner to sell, and no change to your ad stack.
- **The duplicated editorial work, gone.** When several agencies move on one event, the system collapses the near-identical wires into one, picks the strongest, and drafts a rewrite in your voice. **Five reads of one dispatch become one.** Editors put the saved hours into original work.
- **Your whole archive, queryable in plain language.** "What did we report on this MP the last time this came up?" stops being an afternoon of grepping and returns an answer in seconds, for the junior as readily as the veteran.

## I build the inventory, your partner sells it

I build the tech layer that creates more owned inventory and saves editorial time: meaning-based linking, wire clustering, an in-CMS assistant. I don't run header bidding, yield or any ad operations, and I don't touch your commercial setup. I hand your ad partner more to sell, and they monetize it.

Second boundary, just as firm: nothing publishes itself. The journalist keeps the final click, because the byline is the whole asset.

## Who this is for

A newsroom on wire feeds, obviously. Any outlet with a deep archive earning nothing, a content team reworking the same sources as every rival, or a publisher whose pages per visit sit near one.
